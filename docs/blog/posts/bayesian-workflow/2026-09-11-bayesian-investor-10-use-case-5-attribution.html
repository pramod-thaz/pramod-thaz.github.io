Use Case 5 — Performance & Risk Measurement and Attribution: The Scorekeeper

What it does. Every firm must answer two questions at the end of each period: where did our return come from? and where does our risk hide? This use case decomposes the portfolio’s return into its sources (which decisions added value, which just rode the market) and decomposes its risk into its contributors (which positions are the hazards), each with an honest confidence level.

The method. (A family of Bayesian attribution methods — see the catalog below for the specific method attached to each question, from Bayesian linear regression for return attribution through Bayesian risk decomposition for risk attribution.)

The analogy — the kitchen audit. After a great dinner service, the owner wants to know: was the success the menu (asset allocation), the cooking (security selection), or just the crowd (the market being in a good mood)? A naive look at the takings can’t separate these. The audit traces each dish back to its ingredients and each decision to its contribution — and, crucially, says how sure it is. “The menu added value” is one thing. “The menu added value, but we can’t actually be sure the crowd didn’t do it all” is a different, more honest, and more useful sentence.

The analogy — the fire-safety inspection. Risk is like fire hazard: you need to know where the risk lives, not just that “there is risk.” The inspection walks the building (the portfolio) and reports which rooms (positions) hold the most combustible material and how much of the total hazard each contributes. Some hazards are obvious (a big concentrated bet); some are sneaky (a cluster of positions that all catch fire together — a correlation risk that no single position reveals).

Why it matters for investing. This is the accountability engine of the firm. It tells you whether your process genuinely adds value or just rides the market’s tide, and it tells you where to concentrate risk-reduction effort. With confidence attached, it prevents both overconfidence (“we’re geniuses”) and false modesty (“we’re lucky”).

Why it beats the traditional approach. Traditional attribution gives point estimates — a single “alpha” number — with no sense of how much that number should be trusted. The Bayesian version separates skill from luck with an honest uncertainty band, decomposes risk into its true contributors (including hidden correlation risk), and knows when the honest answer is “too uncertain to say.”

Performance & Risk Attribution and Decomposition: A Focused Catalog

Here is a catalog of the scorekeeper’s tools — each answering one specific question, each with its analogy and its payoff. All of these are variations on the same Bayesian move: estimate, decompose, and attach an honest confidence level. The first group covers the core measurement questions; the second extends into timing, style, costs, and the deeper decomposition of both return and risk.

The common thread. Every row is the same bookmaker’s move, pointed at the scorekeeper’s job: take a number that looks like a single fact, pull it apart into its honest components, and attach a confidence to every piece — estimate the whole distribution, decompose it, and never report a single number without its band. That is the discipline this roadmap builds — and it applies just as cleanly to deciding how to split your portfolio as to explaining what last quarter actually earned.

Where the Use Cases Plug Into the Roadmap

  • Use Cases 1 and 4 are portfolio-construction applications of Phases 1 and 2 (beliefs, models, and sampling) — building and allocating the book. Use Case 4 also draws on Phase 3 (the GPS/unsmoothing machinery for private assets) and Phase 4 (regime-awareness and the views channel) to run the full allocation operating system.
  • Use Case 3 is the Phase 4 sensor-fusion and regime-switching machinery, applied to the hardest reporting problem: private-asset stress.
  • Use Case 2 is a focused, high-conviction application of the Phase 2 modeling workflow to a one-off decision (IPO participation).
  • Use Case 5 and the catalog are the Phase 2 alpha/beta discipline scaled into the firm’s full performance- and risk-reporting engine.

Every use case is one idea — start with a belief, update it relentlessly, and never lose sight of how sure you are — wearing a different hat. Your team now has both the machinery and the map of where to point it.
