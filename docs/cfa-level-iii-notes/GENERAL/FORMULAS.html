Asset Allocation

1. CME I

  • Taylor rule (optimal short-term real rate form used in source):
    $$R_{\text{optimal, real}} = R_{\text{neutral}} + 0.5 \times GDP_{g,\text{forecast}} - GDP_{g,\text{trend}} + 0.5 \times (I_{\text{forecast}} - I_{\text{target}})$$

  • Taylor rule (target nominal policy rate form):
    $i^{*} = r_{\text{neutral}} + \pi_{e} + 0.5(\hat{Y}{e} - \hat{Y}{\text{trend}}) + 0.5(\pi_{e} - \pi_{\text{target}})$

  • National income identity linking net exports:
    $(X - M) = $S - I$ + (T - G)$

[FORMULAS IN MOCKS]

  • Taylor rule (target real short-term policy rate):
    $r^* = r_{\text{neutral}} + 0.5(Y_e - Y_{\text{trend}}) + 0.5(\pi_e - \pi_{\text{target}})$
    (Nominal target rate adds expected inflation; $Y_e$ is expected real GDP growth = nominal GDP growth − expected inflation.)

  • Long-run real GDP growth:
    $\text{Real GDP growth} = \text{growth from labor productivity} + \text{growth from labor inputs}$

  • Debt-to-GDP ratio:
    $\dfrac{\text{Short-term external debt} + \text{Medium- and long-term external debt}}{\text{Nominal GDP}}$

  • Foreign-exchange reserves / short-term debt:
    $\dfrac{\text{FX reserves}}{\text{Short-term external debt}}$

2. CME - II

  • Grinold–Kroner expected equity return:
    $E[R_e] \approx D/P + $%\Delta E - %\Delta S$ + %\Delta P/E$
    where $%\Delta E$ (nominal earnings growth) = real GDP growth + long-term inflation + corporate earnings growth premium; $%\Delta S$ = change in shares outstanding (negative for buy-backs).

  • CAPM beta:
    $\beta_i = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(R_M)}$

  • Singer–Terhaar risk premium (fully integrated):
    $RP_i = \sigma_i \times \rho_{i,M} \times (\text{Sharpe ratio of GIM})$

  • Singer–Terhaar risk premium (fully segmented):
    $RP_i = \sigma_i \times (\text{Sharpe ratio})$

  • Real-estate expected return:
    $E(R_{re}) = \text{CapRate} + \text{NOI growth rate} - %\Delta\text{CapRate}$
    (NOI growth includes real growth + inflation; a decline in cap rate contributes positively).

  • Number of distinct covariance elements in an $N$-asset VCV matrix:
    $\frac{N(N-1)}{2}$

    (NOI growth rate is typically real NOI growth + inflation.)

3. Overview of AA

Economic net worth: $$ \text{Economic Net Worth} = \text{Total Economic Assets} - \text{Total Economic Liabilities} $$ where Total Economic Assets = Financial assets + Extended assets (e.g., human capital, PV of pledged contributions); Total Economic Liabilities = Financial liabilities + Extended liabilities (e.g., PV of future obligations, consumption needs, specific goals).

Comparative performance ratios (from allocation evaluation): $$ \text{Ratio 1} = \frac{(k - RF)}{\sigma} $$ $$ \text{Ratio 2} = \frac{(k - k_T)}{\sigma} $$ $$ \text{Ratio 3} = \frac{(k - RF)}{\beta} $$ where $k$ = expected annual return, $RF$ = risk-free return, $k_T$ = target return, $\sigma$ = annual volatility, $\beta$ = CAPM beta.

Sharpe ratio (used for ranking allocations): $$ \text{Sharpe ratio} = \frac{\text{Expected return} - \text{Risk-free rate}}{\text{Expected volatility}} $$

[FORMULAS IN MOCKS]

  • Economic net worth = Total economic assets − Total economic liabilities
    (Economic assets include financial assets + human capital/PV of earnings + residence + other extended assets; liabilities include mortgage + PV of future consumption + other extended liabilities.)

4. Principles of AA

Safety-first / probability ratio (for ranking portfolios against a required threshold return $R_L$): $$ \frac{E(R_P) - R_L}{\sigma_P} $$

Utility / objective function in mean-variance optimization (returns and variance in percent form): $$ U_m = E(R_{s,m}) - 0.005 \lambda \sigma^2(R_{s,m}) $$ $or equivalently $E(R) - 0.005\lambda\sigma^2$$

Marginal contribution to total risk $MCTR$: $$ \text{MCTR}i = \beta{i,p} \times \sigma_p $$

Absolute contribution to total risk $ACTR$: $$ \text{ACTR}_i = w_i \times \text{MCTR}_i $$

For an optimal portfolio the ratio of excess return to MCTR equals the portfolio Sharpe ratio: $$ \frac{E(R_p) - R_f}{\text{MCTR}_i} = \frac{E(R_p) - R_f}{\sigma_p} $$

Funding ratio: $$ \text{Funding ratio} = \frac{\text{Market value of assets}}{\text{Present value of liabilities}} $$

Reverse-optimization / CAPM equilibrium return: $$ E(R_i) = R_f + \beta_i \times \text{Market risk premium} $$

Inflation-adjusted annual cash-flow (growing-annuity form used for living-expenditure goals): $$ \text{Cash flow} = \text{Capital} \times \frac{k - g}{1 - \left(\frac{1+g}{1+k}\right)^n} \times \frac{1}{1+k} $$ (where $k$ = required success-rate return, $g$ = inflation)

Present-value funding of a future goal: $$ \text{PV} = \frac{\text{FV}}{(1 + k)^n} $$ (where $k$ is the minimum expected return consistent with the required success probability)

Portfolio Construction

Overview of Equity

  • Explicit trading costs = Brokerage commissions + Transaction taxes + Exchange fees + Regulatory fees
    (expressed in percent or basis points converted to percent)

  • Herfindahl–Hirschman Index $HHI$ = $\sum w_i^2$
    where $w_i$ are the portfolio weights (including the sum of squared weights of remaining holdings)

  • Effective number of stocks = $\dfrac{1}{\text{HHI}}$

Overview of Fixed Income

  • Coupon income = $\dfrac{\text{Annual average coupon payment}}{\text{Current average bond price}}$

  • Rolldown return = $\dfrac{\text{Expected average bond price at end of horizon} - \text{Current average bond price}}{\text{Current average bond price}}$
    (assumes unchanged yield curve over the strategy horizon)

  • Rolling yield = Coupon income + Rolldown return

  • Expected change in price based on yield or spread view ≈ $(- \text{Modified Duration} \times \Delta y) + \left[\frac{1}{2} \times \text{Convexity} \times (\Delta y)^{2}\right]$

  • Leveraged portfolio return = $\dfrac{(\text{Return on invested funds} \times \text{Value of portfolio assets}) - (\text{Borrowing rate} \times \text{Value of borrowed funds})}{\text{Value of portfolio equity}}$

  • Total expected return = Rolling yield $+/-$ $E$(Change in price based on investor’s benchmark yield view) $+/-$ $E$(Change in price due to investor’s view of credit spread) $+/-$ $E$(Currency gains or losses)

  • Level of inflation protection for coupons = Percentage of portfolio in floating-coupon bonds + Percentage of portfolio in inflation-linked bonds

Trading

  • Inside (market) bid–ask spread = Highest bid price − Lowest ask price

  • Effective bid–ask spread (buy order) = $2 \times \bigl(\text{Trade price} - \dfrac{\text{Ask price} + \text{Bid price}}{2}\bigr)$
    or equivalently $2 \times (\text{Trade price} - \text{Midpoint of the market at order entry})$

  • Average effective spread = $\dfrac{\sum \text{Effective spreads of individual trades}}{N}$

  • Volume-weighted average execution price for a multi-level fill against the book = $\dfrac{\sum (\text{Size}_i \times \text{Price}_i)}{\text{Total shares}}$

  • Effective-spread transaction cost per share (midquote benchmark) = $\dfrac{\text{Trade price} - \text{Prevailing bid}}{2}$ (for buys)
    Total transaction cost estimate = Effective-spread cost per share $\times$ Total shares traded

Performance

Portfolio Performance Evaluation

Brinson-Hood-Beebower $BHB$ selection effect:
$S_i = W_i \times (R_i - B_i)$

BHB allocation effect:
$A_i = $w_i - W_i$ \times B_i$

BHB interaction effect:
$I_i = $w_i - W_i$ \times (R_i - B_i)$

Brinson-Fachler allocation effect:
$A_i = $w_i - W_i$ \times (B_i - B)$

where $w_i$ = portfolio weight in sector $i$, $W_i$ = benchmark weight in sector $i$, $R_i$ = portfolio return in sector $i$, $B_i$ = benchmark return in sector $i$, $B$ = overall benchmark return.

Combined selection + interaction:
$W_i(R_i - B_i) + (w_i - W_i)(R_i - B_i)$

Sortino ratio:
$SR_D = \frac{r_p - r_T}{\sigma_D}$
$$\sigma_D$ = target semi-deviation below target return $r_T$$

Sharpe ratio:
$SR = \frac{r_p - r_f}{\sigma}$

Misfit active return (definition): difference between broad market benchmark return and manager’s normal portfolio return.

Overview of the Global Investment Performance Standards

Modified Dietz sub-period return with external cash flow:
$$ $r = \frac{EMV - BMV - CF}{BMV + \sum (CF_i \times w_i)}$ $$
where $w_i$ is the weighting factor (proportion of the period the cash flow was in the portfolio). Sub-period returns are geometrically linked to obtain the full-period return.

Composite return (asset-weighted):
$$ $r_{comp} = \sum (w_p \times r_p)$ $$
where $w_p$ is the weight of each portfolio based on beginning assets (or beginning assets plus weighted cash flows).

Option Strategies

  • Collar position delta:
    $$\text{Collar delta}=\Delta_{\text{stock}}+\Delta_{\text{put}}-\Delta_{\text{call}}$$

  • Synthetic long stock (put-call parity):
    $$S_0=c_0-p_0+\frac{X}{(1+r)^T}$$

  • Synthetic short stock:
    $$-S_0=p_0-c_0-\frac{X}{(1+r)^T}$$

  • Protective-put maximum loss:
    $$\text{Max loss}=S_0-X+p_0$$

  • Protective-put breakeven:
    $$\text{Breakeven}=S_0+p_0$$

  • Bull-call-spread maximum profit:
    $$\text{Max profit}=(X_H-X_L)-(c_L-c_H)$$

  • Bull-call-spread breakeven:
    $$\text{Breakeven}=X_L+(c_L-c_H)$$

  • Bear-put-spread net cost / maximum loss:
    $$\text{Net cost}=p_H-p_L$$
    Maximum loss occurs when underlying $\ge X_H$.

  • Straddle / strangle cost and breakevens:
    $$\text{Cost}=c+p$$
    Long-straddle breakevens $=X\pm\text{cost}$
    Long-strangle upper breakeven $=X_{\text{call}}+\text{cost}$

  • Covered-call effective sale price:
    $$\text{Effective sale price}=X+c_0$$
    Breakeven $=S_0-c_0$

  • Covered-call position delta:
    $$\text{Position delta}=(1-\Delta_{\text{call}})\times\text{shares}$$

  • Variance of option gamma: highest for ATM options near expiration.

Swaps, Forwards, and Futures Strategies

  • Basis-point-value hedge ratio (bond futures):
    $$BPVHR=\left{\frac{BPV_T-BPV_P}{BPV_{CTD}}\right}\times CF$$
    where $$BPV_T=MDUR_T\times0.0001\times MV_P$$ and $$BPV_P=MDUR_P\times0.0001\times MV_P$$ (or given directly).

  • Equity-index futures contracts for beta adjustment or equitization:

Fixed-Income Active Management: Credit Strategies

$$ $\text{YTM}(i) = \text{YTM(Treasury)} + \text{Yield Spread}(i)$ $$

Expected excess return / excess spread (relative value analysis)
EXR ≈ (s × t) − (Δs × SD) − (t × p × L)
or
Expected excess spread ≈ Spread₀ − (EffSpreadDur × ΔSpread) − (POD × LGD)
where s/Spread₀ = credit spread (Z-spread or OAS), t = holding period (years), SD/EffSpreadDur = spread duration, Δs/ΔSpread = expected change in spread, p/POD = probability of default, L/LGD = loss severity/given default.

Active share
Active share = ½ × Σ |w_{portfolio,i} − w_{benchmark,i}| for i = 1 to N

Fundamental law of active management
E(R_A) = IC × √BR × σ_RA × TC
where IC = information coefficient, BR = breadth (# independent investment decisions/year), σ_RA = active risk (tracking error), TC = transfer coefficient.

Approximate bond price change
ΔP/P ≈ −MD × Δy or −EffSpreadDur × Δs

G-spread
G-spread = bond YTM − linearly interpolated on-the-run government YTM (weights based on maturity distance to bracketing gov bonds)

I-spread
I-spread = bond YTM − linearly interpolated swap rate (swap rate = gov YTM + swap spread for same tenor)

Asset swap spread $ASW$
ASW ≈ bond coupon − interpolated swap rate (same maturity)

CDS upfront payment / initial price
Upfront (% notional) ≈ EffSpreadDur_CDS × (market spread − standard coupon)
CDS price (per 100 notional) ≈ 100 − upfront

VaR / tail risk scaling
Horizon yield change (z-score) ≈ daily yield volatility × z × √trading days
Position loss ≈ notional × (price/100) × (−MD × Δy)
